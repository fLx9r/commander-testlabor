Questions and answers

How are the decks tested?

The 64 most-played commanders on EDHREC play against each other in the Forge simulator, four players per table, piloted by the Forge AI. Each deck plays about 10 games per tournament in a Swiss system. New tournaments run regularly, mostly at night, 1 so far.

Where do the decks come from?

They are based on the decklists of thousands of players on EDHREC. The deck builder picks the strongest deck within the budget, makes sure there is enough ramp, card draw, removal, board wipes and protection, and improves it with the results from Forge. 100 cards, color identity and the official banlist are always checked.

How is a requested deck made?

For example Kaalia up to €250:

  1. Build: The deck builder builds it exactly like the tournament decks: from the EDHREC lists for your commander, with Cardmarket prices, within budget (±€10) and with at most 3 Game Changers for limited budgets. Everything the program has learned about individual cards from all tournaments so far is included.
  2. Optimize, about 3 hours: The deck plays four-player games in Forge against the strongest known decks in the same budget. Each round tests up to 6 versions with a few swapped cards, 14 games each. Whatever wins more often is kept.
  3. Confirm: Finally the starting deck and the improved deck play 42 more games each. Only changes confirmed there stay in.
  4. Deliver: You get the decklist, a Cardmarket shopping list and the win rate by email. The deck only appears on this site if you agreed.

Requested decks don't play in the ranking tournaments. Their win rate comes from games against especially strong opponents, so it can't be compared with the rankings.

What counts towards the budget?

All 100 cards including the commander, at the Cardmarket average price (updated daily). The standard classes are up to €200 and no limit. To keep price changes from pushing a deck out of its class, a deviation of up to €10 is allowed.

How strong are the decks?

Up to €200: at most 3 Game Changers (Bracket 3). Game Changers are especially powerful cards from the official Wizards of the Coast list; brackets describe how strong a Commander deck is. In the no-limit class: any number of Game Changers (Bracket 4). Combos are allowed; the official banlist applies. Only the number of Game Changers is checked; other bracket guidelines (such as no early two-card combos) are not checked by the deck builder.

What does the status mean?

Above average or Below average means: with 95% confidence stronger or weaker than an average deck, which wins 25% at a four-player table. Undecided means: there aren't enough games yet to tell.

Does the AI play like a human?

No. The Forge AI can't play 2,525 cards at all. In tournaments they are replaced by similar cards but stay on the shopping list. Another 2,108 it plays only partially well. The rankings are a good guide, not a final verdict.

Why do the decks change?

The program learns from every game. In each tournament every deck tries out one card: a weaker card is swapped for an almost equally strong alternative, sometimes for a Game Changer while the limit allows it. If the new card wins more, its rating goes up and it shows up in the deck more often. If it doesn't help, it is dropped again.

The same swap is only retried after three weeks. The deck page lists what was tested under “Adjusted for the tournament”. The shopping list stays the proven version.

How current is the data?

Prices and EDHREC data are loaded daily, combos (Commander Spellbook) weekly, card data (Scryfall) every 30 days. The site is rebuilt after every tournament and once a day.
